# The chronicler: testimony, limited time and the record

Design for GitHub issue #4 (ideas 1, 2 and 4 in `docs/ai/IDEAS.md`), worked
through one chapter. Built in November 2026 (see "As built" at the end). Read `AGENTS.md` and
`docs/content-guide.md` first; their story rules apply to every line below.

## Decision

- **Chapter:** Fort Shaykh Ṭabarsí (`tabarsi`). Nabíl's two Ṭabarsí chapters
  (XIX and XX, pp. 324 to 430) name their informants, say who heard what
  through whom, check a rumour against an eyewitness, attribute a count to
  the man who kept it, and end by admitting the record is incomplete. The
  Tabríz trial has fewer named chains (ʿAẓím, Dr Cormick, and the
  translator's notes on official accounts) and a single room, so it can
  wait for a second pass.
- **The day:** one undated day in February or early March 1849, after
  Mullá Ḥusayn's burial and before Naw-Rúz. Nabíl puts three things in it
  (p. 384): Mírzá Muḥammad-Báqir shares out the rice Mullá Ḥusayn had
  stored, Quddús tells anyone afraid to leave now, and that same night
  Siyyid Mírzá Ḥusayn-i-Mutavallí writes to ʿAbbás-Qulí Khán, and Siyyid
  ʿAlíy-i-Zargar slips out at midnight with the letter and his share of
  rice (pp. 384 to 385). No fighting happens that day, the dead are still
  being counted, and the fort is full of people who disagree.
- **The loop:** dawn is fixed (the rice). Then the player can reach three of
  six places before nightfall. Night is fixed (the watch at the gate). By
  the keeper's last wick the player writes the record: for each question,
  whose account goes in and how sure they are. The record is sealed when the
  chapter ends and is read back as the player's testimony on the exile road
  in chapter 10.
- **No new verbs of violence.** Every place is listening, carrying,
  binding or keeping. History cannot be changed. What changes is what the
  player saw, what they wrote down, and what they chose not to write.

## What Nabíl records about disagreement (the real instances)

These are the patterns the mechanic copies. Each is something Nabíl actually
does in these chapters.

1. **A rumour checked against an eyewitness.** In Ṭihrán, a month after the
   siege, Nabíl hears Mírzá Aḥmad tell of Mullá Ḥusayn's sword-stroke at
   Bárfurúsh. Later, in Mashhad, he asks Mírzá Muḥammad-i-Furúghí, who says:
   "Had I not seen it with my own eyes, I never would have believed it"
   (pp. 331 to 332).
2. **A court chronicle refuted by a classmate.** The *Násikhu't-Taváríkh*
   says Mullá Ḥusayn trained with the sword in his youth. Furúghí, who knew
   him from childhood, calls it "sheer fabrication": his hand trembled when
   he wrote (p. 333; ص ۲۹۹).
3. **Testimony in layers.** Mullá Ṣádiq and Furúghí were sent out of the
   room where the dying Mullá Ḥusayn lay with Quddús. They heard his voice
   answering for two hours. Mírzá Muḥammad-Báqir watched "through a fissure
   in the door" and heard two sentences. Nabíl has the scene from Ṣádiq and
   Furúghí, who have part of it from Báqir (p. 381; ص ۳۴۷).
4. **A count with a name on it.** "The number of martyrs, according to the
   computation of Mírzá Muḥammad-Báqir, had reached a total of seventy-two"
   (p. 382).
5. **Third-hand, and said so.** Mullá Mihdí of Kand on the wall, refusing
   to come home to his infant son Raḥmán: Nabíl has it from Áqáy-i-Kalím,
   who had it from the messenger himself, in Ṭihrán (pp. 397 to 398).
6. **The other side's account.** The *Taríkh-i-Jadíd* (fn 45, p. 413) has
   ʿAbbás-Qulí Khán, years later, telling a prince that the soldiers wept to
   hear Mullá Ḥusayn and that he ordered them to shout to drown his voice.
   It reaches Nabíl's translator through Mírzá Jání, from "one who, though
   not a believer, was honest". Nabíl's own text says the Khán did not know
   whom he had shot (p. 380) and learned it only from the traitor's letter,
   which he then kept "even from his closest officers" (pp. 385 to 386).
7. **Counts that do not line up.** 312 counted through the gate, then a
   youth from Bárfurúsh made 313 (p. 354). 313 rode out on the last night
   (p. 379). Gobineau says "three hundred" (fn 49, p. 366). 202 rode to
   Vás-Kas (p. 365) and 202 walked out under the oath (p. 400).
8. **An admitted gap.** "As to those believers who participated in that
   memorable siege and survived its tragic end, I have been thus far unable
   to ascertain in full either their names or their number" (p. 429). His
   names came "from various sources", chiefly Ismu'lláhu'l-Mím,
   Ismu'lláhu'l-Javád and Ismu'lláhu'l-Asad (pp. 413 to 414).

Note on names in the brief: Siyyid Mírzá Ḥusayn-i-Mutavallí is not one of
Nabíl's informants. He is the deserter who wrote to the Khán (p. 384), left
the fort the day before the oath (p. 398) and later struck the bound Quddús
at Bárfurúsh (pp. 411 to 412). Mírzá Ḥaydar-ʿAlí and Ḥájí Naṣír are not
informants in these chapters either. Ḥájí Naṣír appears only as a captive
ransomed to Ṭihrán, with Furúghí (p. 404).

## The questions (claims)

Six questions. Each has two to four accounts, each tied to a page. The
player's own account exists only where they were actually there.

1. **`ch8-fallen` How many have fallen since Bárfurúsh?** Báqir: seventy-two,
   thirty-six of them in one grave on the north side (p. 382). Riḍá: "I
   counted thirty-six carried north." Ṣádiq: near ninety wounded that night,
   most will not rise (p. 382; ص ۳۴۸). Conscript: "Hundreds of them, the
   officers say." Riḍá corroborates Báqir, which is the lesson: two
   independent accounts earn "sure".
2. **`ch8-grave` Where does Mullá Ḥusayn lie?** Quddús told the seven
   who laid him to rest to hide the place "even from their companions"
   (p. 382), and the player was at the gate, not among them. Keeper: "They
   turned the earth on the south side, against the wall" (p. 381). Riḍá:
   "Inside, by the Shaykh himself, they say" (fn 6, p. 381; ص ۳۴۸). Báqir:
   "Quddús asked it. Write that he lies near the Shaykh, and no nearer."
   Withholding is offered and means something here.
3. **`ch8-door` What passed behind the closed door?** Own: two voices, and
   then one (the existing `ch8-mh-last` scene). Ṣádiq and Furúghí: his voice
   answering, for two hours. Báqir: the two sentences, "Are you well pleased
   with me?" among them (p. 381). Withholding is offered: Quddús called the
   words confidential. In chapter 1 the player sat outside another closed
   door with Mullá Ḥusayn inside; the record can say so.
4. **`ch8-khan` Did the man in the tree know whom he shot?** A Wounded
   Believer (existing): a shot from the branches, into the firelight
   (p. 380). Conscript: the Khán does not even know he hit him (pp. 380,
   385). Later, after the Khán's attack, the conscript's existing boast
   ("he says he aimed at him") becomes a third, hindsight account.
5. **`ch8-sword` Was Mullá Ḥusayn a trained swordsman?** Conscript: the
   camp says he trained for years (the *Násikhu't-Taváríkh*, p. 333).
   Furúghí: never; his hand trembled when he wrote (p. 333). Own: you sat
   beside him at Karbilá and remember the pen shaking (the player is his
   fellow student; this corroborates Furúghí and invents nothing new).
6. **`ch8-midnight` Why did a man go out of the gate at midnight?** Seen at
   the night watch, every playthrough. Riḍá: "Hungry. Quddús said whoever
   was afraid should go." Own (only if the player met the Siyyid from Qum):
   he came from the siyyid's lean-to, and the siyyid had asked all day
   where Mullá Ḥusayn lies. Later, when the Khán attacks before Naw-Rúz,
   Báqir brings Quddús's words: "The betrayer has announced the death of
   Mullá Ḥusayn to ʿAbbás-Qulí Khán" (p. 386). The claim stays open until
   the chapter ends, so the player can revise it.

Left out on purpose: the Bárfurúsh sword-stroke ("six pieces", p. 332 and
fn 45) and Mullá Ḥusayn's battles, which would make the record about
killing; and the length of the hunger (ʿAbdu'l-Bahá's "eighteen days"
in fn 27, p. 395, against the *Taríkh-i-Jadíd*'s "three months"), because a
statement of ʿAbdu'l-Bahá should not be set up as one witness to weigh
against others.

**What is missed.** Three of six places cover at most four claims well.
Some pairs pull against each other: the stream gives the other side, the
lean-to gives the midnight connection, and both are outside the fort's
centre. Missing a place leaves its accounts unheard, and the record says
"I did not hear this" for any question with none. A replay of the chapter
(chapter select resets every `heard:ch8-*` and `rec:ch8-*` key) shows the
other half.

**How time passes.** A visit counts when the place's last node sets its
flag. Talking and walking away costs nothing. Slots are counted from the
flags, not stored, so no counter can drift:

- the objective "Before nightfall, go where you are needed" uses
  `progress` for (n/3);
- `ambience` darkens with the visit count (daylight, then the dusk wash
  `0xd08850` at 0.3, then the night wash);
- once three are visited, every unvisited place swaps through `gate` to
  one shared tree, `ch8-day-late`: "The light has gone. Riḍá is waiting at
  the gate."

On the third visit, its first node warns: "The light is going. After this,
the gate."

## The record

The record is a page of the journal, one per chapter that has claims. For
each question the player picks:

- **whose account** goes in (only accounts they heard), or **both** (the
  disagreement itself, Nabíl's own move), or **withheld** (only on `grave`
  and `door`);
- **how sure** they are: *sure*, *likely* or *unsure*.

How the player came by an account (saw, told by one who saw, rumour) is
fixed by the data and shown beside it, the way Nabíl writes "according to
the computation of". The player judges; they never relabel a rumour as
something they saw. Questions with no account heard show the claim's
`unheard` line and cannot be edited.

The record can be changed from the journal until `tabarsiComplete`, then
it is sealed. The night scene at the wick opens it for the six questions,
with a narrator line before and after.

## Data shapes

`src/data/record/`: `types.ts`, `claims08.ts` (the six questions),
`keys.ts`, `day.ts`, `page.ts`, `testimony.ts`, `carried.ts`. Player-facing
strings sit in `text:` / `title:` fields so the localization sync finds them.

```ts
export type Provenance = 'saw' | 'told' | 'rumour';     // fixed by the data
export type Confidence = 'sure' | 'likely' | 'unsure';  // the player's judgement
export interface Line { text: string }

export interface Account {
  id: string;            // 'baqir'
  speaker: string;       // a dialogue speaker string, or 'You'
  provenance: Provenance;
  text: string;          // what the record holds if this account is chosen
  agrees?: string[];     // accounts that bear it out (two may be written as sure)
  source: string;        // 'Nabíl p. 382; ص ۳۴۸', for writers
}
export interface Claim {
  id: string; chapter: ChapterId; title: string; accounts: Account[];
  both: Line; withheld?: Line; unheard: Line;
}
export interface ChapterDay {
  id: string; opens: (f: FlagState) => boolean; closes: string; slots: number;
  places: { id: string; flag: string }[];
}
```

Flags stay in the flat `FlagState`: `heard:<claim>:<account>` (built with
`heard(claim, account)`, which throws on an unknown claim or account, so a
typo fails as the game loads), `rec:<claim>` (an account id, `both` or
`withheld`) and `conf:<claim>`. Visits are counted from the place flags
(`visits`, `canVisit`, `nightfall` in `day.ts`), never stored.

Dialogue additions (`src/dialogue/types.ts`):

- `DialogueChoice.write?: string[]`: opens `RecordScene` for those claims,
  then goes on to `next`, the way `play` runs a mini-game.
- `DialogueChoice.present?: { claim, account }`: shown only once that
  account is heard; drawn in teal with a teal edge.
- `DialogueChoice.voice?: Voice` and `DialogueNode.voice?: NodeVoice | NodeVoice[]`
  (`Voice = 'doubt' | 'fear' | 'longing'`): a voiced choice, or a voice cutting
  in after the text, drawn in the voice's colour with its name. Heeding and
  resisting are recorded with `heed(moment, voice)` / `resist(moment)` from
  `src/dialogue/voices.ts` as `voice:<moment>`; `heededAt`, `resistedAt` and
  `heededCount` read them.
- `DialogueNode.lines?: (f) => string[]`: paragraphs after `text`, each a
  literal from game data (the last-light warning, Ṣádiq's memory, the
  testimony).

`MapRemark.effect?: FlagOp` is applied when a remark is said, so Riḍá's
remarks while walking can set `heard:` keys.

## The epilogue: testimony

On the exile road (`ch10-final`, `src/data/dialogues_ch/chapter10_exile_road.ts`)
the `hidden` node gains one choice, "(Say what you saw.)", into new nodes
spliced into the same tree; its four closing choices move to a node
`answer`. The player picks up to three sealed questions to tell
(choices `requires` a `rec:` key and not yet told). Each answer node is a
Narrator line naming the question, plus `lines: f => recordLines(claim, f)`
(the recorded sentence, then one short confidence sentence: "I am sure of
it.", "So I believe.", "I could not settle it.").

Two closing lines branch on the record, then the existing `answer` node:

- any `withheld`: "One thing you do not say. Quddús asked it, and you kept it."
- two or more questions unheard: "Some of it you did not see. You say so,
  and leave room for those who did." (Nabíl, p. 429.)

New flag `ch10Testimony: 'kept' | 'gaps' | 'full'`. Nabíl is not placed at
the gate: he was not there, and he began writing in 1888. The chapter's end
card says so instead: Nabíl-i-Zarandí asked those who had been there, and
where they disagreed, he wrote that down too. This is what you would have
told him.

## How it interlocks

- **Inner voices (#5).** Add `DialogueChoice.voice?: 'doubt' | 'fear' |
  'longing'`, drawn as a coloured lead-in. Ṭabarsí uses all three. Fear,
  at Quddús's dawn warning: "(Fear) The gate will be open tonight. No one
  would blame you." At midnight it returns as Riḍá's hand on your arm.
  Doubt, at the wick: it offers *unsure* first for anything you did not
  see, and *sure* only when two accounts agree (claim 1 is the teaching
  case). Longing comes later, with Mullá Mihdí of Kand and his son Raḥmán
  (pp. 397 to 398, through Áqáy-i-Kalím). The chronicler needs no voice
  strength counter, so `FlagOp.add` waits for #5 itself.
- **Facts carried between conversations (#6).** A heard account is the
  fact. Add `DialogueChoice.present?: { claim: string; account: string }`,
  shown only if heard, rendered as "(Tell him what Furúghí said.)". Three
  uses here: tell the conscript about the trembling pen (he repeats it in
  camp, and his later boast scene remembers); tell Báqir the Khán does not
  know (he says: "Then let it not leave these walls", and that night it
  does); refuse the Siyyid from Qum by presenting Quddús's charge of
  secrecy.
- **Care, not combat (#7).** Two places are care first (the wounded, the
  water skins), and Ṣádiq talks only while you work. At the wall one man is
  dying: "(Hold his hand and ask nothing.)" ends the visit with no claim
  heard, and the journal keeps it. At midnight: "(Give him your share for
  the road.)" sets `ch8ZargarGiven`. Nobody stops him, because nobody did.
- **Memory across chapters (#8).** Mullá Ṣádiq greets the player from
  Shíráz when `sadiqWitnessed`. `ch8RationFavoured` colours his first line
  at the wounded. The door question can cite chapter 1's vigil. The sealed
  record is the epilogue's testimony, and `ch10Testimony` joins
  `ch10Final` as the ending's state. Records are per chapter, so the Tabríz
  trial can add its own claims later, and the epilogue picks from all of them.
- **Talking while walking (#9).** During the day Riḍá walks with the
  player. Rumour-grade accounts come free through his barks as you pass a
  place ("They say he lies inside, by the Shaykh himself"), while testimony
  costs a visit. Shape, for when the non-blocking mode exists:

  ```ts
  export interface Bark {
    id: string; speaker: string; text: string;
    at: { scene: string; gridX: number; gridY: number; radius: number };
    when: (f: FlagState) => boolean;
    effect?: FlagOp;          // e.g. { set: { [heard('ch8-grave', 'rida')]: true } }
  }
  ```

  Until then they are one-node dialogues on story objects.
